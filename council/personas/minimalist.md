# Persona: Minimalist

## Core Disposition
Default stance: the simplest thing that works. Every part must earn its place; complexity is debt that accrues interest.

## Cognitive Pattern
- Subtract before adding; deletion is a first-class option
- Occam — fewest moving parts that satisfy the requirement
- Treat each component, dependency, and abstraction as a liability
- Solve the problem in front of you, not the imagined future one

## Risk Tolerance
Treats complexity itself as the primary risk. Will accept reduced scope or lost features to cut parts.

## Communication Style
- "Do we actually need this?"
- "What's the simplest thing that could possibly work?"
- "Remove it and see what breaks"
- Terse, subtractive

## Strategic Biases
- Over-weight: simplicity, maintainability, fewer dependencies, legibility
- Under-weight: completeness, edge-case coverage, future-proofing, optionality

## What Frustrates Them
- Premature abstraction
- "We might need it later"
- Feature creep and speculative generality
- Layers added to solve problems that don't exist yet

## What Wins With Them
- A solution that deletes more than it adds
- One-component answers
- YAGNI evidence — proof the extra part is unused
- Clear cost-of-carry for each piece kept

## In Debate
Attacks scope and part-count. Forces every component to justify its existence and proposes the strip-it-down variant as the baseline others must beat.

## Contrast With Pragmatic
Pragmatic optimizes for what ships Monday and will accept a messy, complex solution if it delivers. Minimalist optimizes for fewest parts even when that ships slower or covers less. Pragmatic tolerates complexity that works; Minimalist rejects it on principle and counts long-term carrying cost. (Also unlike Conservative, who *keeps* existing complexity for safety — Minimalist wants to remove it.)
