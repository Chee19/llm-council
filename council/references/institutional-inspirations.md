# Institutional Inspirations

The council architecture borrows structures that have worked in human institutions for centuries.

## Source Institutions

| Institution | Borrowed Element |
|---|---|
| Executive councils | Chairman + advisory panel structure |
| Scientific peer review | Anonymous evaluation, evidence-based survival |
| Adversarial legal systems | Independent argument, Judge as neutral adjudicator |
| Strategic war councils | Red-team / blue-team divergence, role specialization |
| Medical multidisciplinary review boards | Cross-specialty convergence on patient case |
| Corporate executive teams | Role + functional ownership, escalation paths |
| Policy advisory boards | Diverse stakeholder representation |
| Editorial boards | Synthesis of dissenting expert views |

## Pattern Mappings

### Peer Review → R2/R3
Reviewers see anonymized work, judge on merit, recommend accept/revise/reject. Council mirrors this in R2 cross-visibility with peer rebuttal.

### Adversarial Law → Specialist Argument
Each side argues its strongest case; the Judge weighs both. Specialists do not pretend neutrality — they argue their (role, persona) position.

### Medical Review Board → Cross-Domain Council
On complex cases, multiple specialties convene with one chair. Each contributes domain expertise; the chair synthesizes treatment. Healthcare playbook follows this pattern directly.

### War Council → Persona Diversity
Strategic planning historically used designated devil's-advocate roles. Persona system encodes this systematically rather than ad-hoc.

## What the Council Is NOT

| Not | Because |
|---|---|
| A jury | No equal-vote tally; arguments don't count, they compete |
| A democracy | Majority does not win; reasoning does |
| A focus group | Not measuring preferences; producing decisions |
| A committee | Committees seek consensus; council seeks survival of strongest |
| A debate club | Debate club judges rhetoric; council judges evidence + logic |

## Why Imitate Institutions At All

Human institutions evolved structures that handle:

- Adversarial reasoning under uncertainty
- Multi-stakeholder coordination
- Accountability and dissent preservation
- Decision quality at scale

LLMs alone reproduce single-perspective reasoning. Institutional structure layered over LLMs reproduces the corrective dynamics that institutions developed to handle high-stakes decisions.
